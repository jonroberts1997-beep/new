How the system works

A regenerative kitchen garden is a highly productive food system that feeds you, grows living soil and improves the ecosystem over time. Things come in, the garden turns them into food and life, and what is left over goes back in. Each lap of the cycle leaves the soil richer than before.

What goes in

  • Seed starting. An indoor or outdoor seed-starting setup grows the transplants for the beds.
  • Direct sowing. Many crops go straight into the soil as seed.
  • Soil life. Compost, mulch, worm castings, comfrey and weed teas, compost tea and charged biochar feed the biology that feeds the plants.
  • Biodiversity. Pollinator flowers, beneficial-insect plants, herbs, perennials and wild edges bring in the allies.
  • Water, sun and care. Your time and regular garden walks are part of the system.

The core of the system

  • Growing beds. The heart of the garden, where food is produced. Everything else supports them.
  • Paths. Permanent, so beds never get walked on. Main paths 3 ft, secondary 18 to 24 in.
  • Compost. Ideally two bays, close to the garden. This is what makes it regenerative rather than just productive.
  • Water. Hose or spigot in reach of every bed, plus a dunk barrel or watering station.
  • Boundary. Fence and gates that keep out deer, rabbits and groundhogs.
  • Workspace. A potting bench and tools close at hand.

What comes out

  • Abundant food. Fresh, nourishing harvests through the seasons.
  • Living soil. Soil that builds its own fertility and improves every year.
  • A thriving ecosystem. More pollinators, birds, beneficial insects and soil life.
  • Confidence and skill. Knowing you can grow your own food.
  • Connection. With other people, with community, and with the natural world. A place to learn and gather.
